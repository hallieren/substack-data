## pico's final report

Implemented the fix.

**Root cause:** In multivariate dense polynomial arithmetic, `dmp_mul_ground` recursively multiplied every coefficient by the scalar but did not special-case multiplication by zero. When differentiating over a finite field, derivative coefficients could become zero because the integer multiplier is divisible by the field characteristic (e.g. `K(7)` in `GF(7)`). This produced a non-canonical “zero” coefficient like `[[], [], [], [], [], []]` instead of the canonical multivariate zero `[]`/`[[]]`. Functions such as `dmp_zero_p`/`dmp_strip` did not recognize that malformed shape as zero, leaving a spurious leading coefficient in the derivative representation. That corrupted degree information and later caused `ZeroDivisionError` in operations like `gcd`.

**Change:** In `sympy/polys/densearith.py`, `dmp_mul_ground` now returns the canonical zero polynomial `dmp_zero(u, K)` immediately when the scalar multiplier is zero.

**Verification:** The reported snippet now completes successfully:

```python

[[[SymmetricModularIntegerMod7(2)]], [[]]]
Poly(x, x, y, z, modulus=7)
```
